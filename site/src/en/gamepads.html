A regular gamepad on PC

How to connect and set up a real gamepad: Xbox, PlayStation, Switch and others. Handy alongside Rulik too: one player on a gamepad, another on a phone.

Which gamepad works how

Almost every PC game is built for the Xbox gamepad (the XInput standard), and Windows already has its driver. Other gamepads work out of the box only if they can pretend to be an Xbox pad or the game supports them directly.

GamepadHow it works
Xbox 360, One, SeriesOut of the box, in every game with gamepad support
8BitDo, budget padsOut of the box in X-input mode
DualShock 4, DualSenseOut of the box only in games with PlayStation support. Otherwise through Steam or DS4Windows
Switch ProThrough Steam

Xbox

Wired. Plug in a USB cable and Windows installs the driver itself. That's it.

Bluetooth (Xbox One S and newer, Series):

  1. On the PC: Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device → Bluetooth.
  2. Turn the gamepad on with the Xbox button, then hold the small pair button on top, next to the USB port, until the Xbox button blinks fast.
  3. Pick "Xbox Wireless Controller" from the list.

Xbox Wireless Adapter. Lower latency and up to 8 gamepads. Pairing works the same way: the button on the adapter, then the one on the gamepad. A wireless Xbox 360 pad needs its own receiver and doesn't work over Bluetooth.

PlayStation

Connect with a cable or over Bluetooth. To pair, hold Share and PS on a DualShock 4, or Create and PS on a DualSense, until the light bar blinks. Then there are three options:

  • The game supports PlayStation itself (Sony ports and many newer games). Everything works right away, with the right button icons.
  • Steam. In Steam, open Settings → Controller and turn on PlayStation support. Steam hands the gamepad to the game as an Xbox pad. Works in Steam games, and you can add other games via Games → Add a Non-Steam Game.
  • DS4Windows. For games outside Steam. It creates a virtual Xbox gamepad through the ViGEmBus driver, the same one Rulik uses. If Rulik Server already installed the driver, you don't need to install it again.
Don't run Steam and DS4Windows on the same gamepad at once: the game will see it twice and every press will register two times.

Switch, 8BitDo and others

Switch Pro. Connects with a cable or over Bluetooth (pair button on top). Turn on its support in Steam, same as for PlayStation.

8BitDo and budget gamepads. They usually have two modes: X-input (pretends to be an Xbox pad) and D-input (the old DirectInput). You want X-input: a switch on the case or a button combo at power-on, check the gamepad's manual. In D-input mode many games don't see the gamepad or mix up the buttons.

Testing

Press Win+R and type joy.cpl. The gamepad should be in the list, and under Properties you can watch the buttons and sticks react. If it shows up there but the game doesn't see it, check the game's control settings and restart it with the gamepad connected.

Games without gamepad support

If a game only understands keyboard and mouse, the gamepad's buttons need to turn into key presses:

  • Steam Input: in the game's controller settings, bind keys and the mouse to buttons and sticks.
  • Outside Steam: AntiMicroX (free and open source), JoyToKey or reWASD (paid).
  • Rulik: no gamepad at all, a keyboard layout right on your phone. Step by step: how to make one.

Troubleshooting

  • Buttons are mixed up: the gamepad is in D-input mode. Switch it to X-input or connect it through Steam.
  • The character or camera moves on its own: stick drift. Raise the dead zone in the game or in Steam.
  • Bluetooth lag: use a cable or the adapter, and turn off other Bluetooth devices nearby.
  • The game picks the wrong gamepad: disconnect the extra ones and connect the one you want first, or choose it in the game's settings.
